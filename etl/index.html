More ways real people are reaching further

  • Who in your family knows the story behind the money? Not the balance, the story, the risk somebody took, the years somebody worked double. Members write that down as part of the program, and enrollment is paid.
  • If your giving stopped tomorrow, would anybody notice? That is not meant as a guilt trip. It is a useful test of whether your money is actually pointed at something or just leaking in a few directions.
  • Do you give more when someone asks you face to face than when you plan it out ahead? Almost everybody does. Planned giving is just a way of protecting your generosity from your own schedule.
  • What is the smallest amount you could send without having to think about it? Start there and nowhere else. Most of what our fund does was built out of amounts nobody missed.
  • What did your parents teach you about money on purpose? Most of us learned by watching, which means we also picked up the bad parts. Entrusted Legacy helps you teach deliberately instead.
  • Have you told anyone where the important papers are? That's the smallest possible version of estate stewardship and most people skip it. Come join free and we'll cover the bigger versions too.
  • What happens to your giving after you are gone? Not the causes, the judgment, the sense of when to say yes. Members build a written giving philosophy as part of the paid program, so somebody can carry it.
  • If your family had to run things without you for a month, what would break first? That answer is your starting point. Join the list and we will walk through it together.
  • Have you ever explained to anyone why you save the way you do? Most people have not, and the reasoning dies with them. The free opt-in gets you into rooms where that reasoning gets spoken aloud.
  • Do you know what your parents intended, or only what the paperwork said? Those are frequently different things. We help members write down both. Free to join.
  • What do you want your name attached to twenty years from now? Not the number, the thing. Our members answer that in writing and then we build the giving around the answers.
  • Do the people in your house know what you give to, and why? That answer ends up mattering more than the number in the account. We give together so the reason never has to be guessed at later.
