More ways real people are reaching further

  • Would you rather your money go toward a plaque or a case of workbooks? We do not do plaques. Everything lands in the programs, and we are happy to walk you through where.
  • Would you rather pay for help now or spend the summer catching up? Both cost something. Spring seats are available if you want the cheaper version.
  • Who was checking on you at four in the afternoon on a Tuesday in eighth grade? For a lot of kids the honest answer is nobody, and the hours pile up. C4K puts somebody in that chair.
  • What did somebody do for you that seemed small at the time and turned out not to be? Most of us have one of those. Giving here is how you become that for another person.
  • Would your kid work harder for a stranger than for you? A lot of them do, and it says nothing bad about you. Use it. Book a session and let somebody else be the one who checks the work.
  • Ever notice how much of a kid's confidence rides on one subject? Fix the math and suddenly they are a different person in every class. We see it constantly at C4K, and you can opt in to hear more.
  • Nine of the kids on our current roster came to us because an older sibling had been here first. Word of mouth in a neighborhood is how this grows, and growth is the thing we can least afford. Funding a seat here is literally funding a seat.
  • We turn the lights on at three fifteen every weekday, whether four kids show up or fourteen. That predictability is the product. Paying for it is less exciting than paying for a new initiative and considerably more useful.
  • Once a month is easier for us to plan around than once a year, if you ever have a choice in the matter. Twenty dollars on a schedule does more for our scheduling than two hundred as a surprise. Both are welcome; I just will not pretend they are the same.
  • Our tutors are mostly college students and retired teachers, which is a lovely combination and a fragile one. Students graduate and retirees travel. Small stipends and stocked materials are what keep the schedule from collapsing in between.
  • Our roster has more sixth graders on it this year than any other grade, which caught me off guard. Middle school is where a lot of kids quietly decide they are bad at math. Extra hands in that room is what we are raising money for.
  • There is a folding table in a church basement with four chairs around it. That table is one of our tutoring sites, and it runs on borrowed space and donated pencils. A gift of any size keeps it stocked through spring.
