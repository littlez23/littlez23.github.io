More ways real people are reaching further

  • Compound interest came up last month in a session that was supposed to be about percentages. We worked through it with a calculator and a legal pad. Fifty dollars is roughly what that kind of supply costs us for a term.
  • What happens on Tuesday if nobody sponsors the supplies? The group meets anyway, with a thinner shelf and more sharing. Two hundred dollars is what a full month looks like instead.
  • Last October we ran the program for a few weeks on donated printer paper from an office that was moving out. It worked, and it also was not a plan. Buying the spring paper and workbooks outright is a two hundred dollar item.
  • I am much better at running programs than I am at pitching them. So here is the plain version: we tutor kids after school and we need people to buy the supplies. Any amount buys something specific.
  • Supplies are the whole reason I am writing, not a vision statement. Forty dollars buys one student's workbook set for a full semester, and that is the smallest thing we sell. Say the word and I will send the invoice.
  • Ask us anything before you enroll and I will answer it straight, including the things we do badly. Some parents want to know staff background checks, some want to know the snack. Both are fair questions.
  • Our staff writes a short note home every two weeks about what your kid actually worked on. No portal, no login, just a paragraph a human wrote. That starts the week you enroll, and enrolling is free.
  • We hold a family night at the start of every session so parents can see the actual room, the actual tables, the actual staff. It is ninety minutes and there is coffee. You can join the list without ever coming to one.
  • A father told us his son had never once described school as fine until December. That is not a metric we can put on a flyer, so I am putting it in a message instead. Free enrollment, spots in three age groups.
  • Sign-ups for our spring tutoring block open Monday and the form takes about two minutes. No cost to join, no obligation after the first session. If it turns out not to be a fit for your kid, you tell us and we close the file.
  • Consistency matters more than intensity with young kids, which is inconvenient for fundraising. Nobody wants to bankroll the eleventh ordinary Tuesday. Those Tuesdays are the whole program, and they need paying for.
  • Half the work of a youth program is just being open on a predictable schedule. Kids will not come back to something that might not be there. Predictability costs money in the form of rent and staff hours.
