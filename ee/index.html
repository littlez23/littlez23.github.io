More ways real people are reaching further

  • Sitting in on one buying call may teach you more than the whole member handbook. That is not a knock on the handbook, which someone spent a long summer writing. It is just how this tends to go.
  • Three questions is all the form is. Nobody has ever complained about the length of it. If it takes you more than a minute, something's broken and I'd like to hear about it.
  • Trust builds slowly and lists build fast. So we start with the fast part and let the rest catch up. Nothing about the opt-in requires you to trust us with anything but a phone number.
  • I'd rather have forty names that answer than four hundred that don't. So the sign-up is deliberately plain and free, and we don't chase anyone. If you're one of the forty, you know it already.
  • Membership does not mean meetings. It means your business is listed among others who'd rather be useful than unreachable. That's the beginning and end of the commitment at this stage.
  • Opting in does not put you on a phone tree or a committee. Nobody will assign you a role. You'll simply be someone the group knows how to reach when the need matches what you have.
  • Our list is still short enough that I can read every name on it in one sitting. That will change, and I'd rather it change with people who actually want to be there. Add yourself if that's you.
  • We are not asking for money in this message, and we won't be next month either. What we want is to know who's out there and what they do. The rest gets figured out when a real need shows up.
  • Charity work is mostly logistics. The warm part shows up later, usually while you are breaking down cardboard in a parking lot at dusk. Earning Enterprises is putting together the next collection run and we need people who do not mind the unglamorous half.
  • A shelter called us asking for alarm clocks. Cheap ones, in bulk, because people were losing jobs over missed shifts and phones that got shut off. That is the kind of request that never makes it into a fundraising letter.
  • Most of giving is showing up before anybody asks. The asking is the hard part for people, and by the time they get there they have usually already tried everything else. Show up early and you skip that whole ordeal for someone.
  • One of our members kept a stack of gas cards in his glovebox for two years and handed them out when a customer looked stretched. He never mentioned it until someone else brought it up. That is roughly the spirit of the EE give lane, just with more people in on it.
