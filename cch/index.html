More ways real people are reaching further

  • Turnover in child welfare work is high and everyone in the field knows it. Volunteers who are trained and supported well stay far longer than that average, which is the only reason a program like ours makes sense. Support costs money, so we ask for it.
  • The word advocate sounds official until you meet one and find out she is a retired postal worker who reads case files at her kitchen table. She was trained, screened, and supported, and that took money. We are raising for the next handful like her.
  • A caseworker handed us a folder last month and said her back seat was full of them. We could take one of those kids on because a hardware store owner had funded a mentor slot in January. That is the whole mechanism, and it is open again.
  • Money buys hours here, nothing else. It pays for the background check, the training weekend, the gas to a court date, and the staff person who calls when a match goes quiet. If you can fund a stretch of those hours, we will put them to work and tell you where they went.
  • Would you rather fund something you can measure or something that matters? We would like to say both, but honestly this work is slow and quiet. If you can live with that, we could use the support.
  • Who in your life would think it was a good idea if you did this? Ask them. Then send whatever you land on to Child Champions Hub, and I will tell you what it covered.
  • Do you know what happens to a mentor match when funding drops mid year? It ends. The kid does not get a memo about budget cycles, so we plan ahead and ask early.
  • Remember the teacher or coach who saw something in you before you did? Somebody is waiting to have that person. Child Champions Hub matches them with adults who opt in, and opting in is free.
  • Three staff members carry the whole operation, and they are the reason a match survives its rough patches. Payroll is the ask nobody likes to make and the one that matters most. Funding a share of a staff month is a purchase we can describe precisely.
  • Thirty case files ride around in a milk crate in the back seat of a county car. The caseworker is not the villain of that story, there simply are not enough hands. Adding your name to our volunteer list is free and takes a minute.
  • Tuesday nights, a retired welder plays chess in a school cafeteria with a nine-year-old who narrates every move. He signed up two years ago knowing nothing about kids. The form he used is still free and still short.
  • Ever been the reason somebody stayed in the room? Our mentors do that on ordinary weekday afternoons, with no audience. Fund one for a year and let somebody else do the showing up.
