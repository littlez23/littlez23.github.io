More ways real people are reaching further

  • Can you name the adult who first took you seriously? Most people answer in about two seconds. We exist for the kids who cannot answer at all, and we need help paying for it.
  • What is your giving actually for? If it is for a plaque on a wall, we are the wrong outfit. If it is for one kid having one adult who keeps coming back, the donate button is on the site.
  • What does forty dollars actually buy here? Background check and onboarding for one new volunteer mentor. Not glamorous, but nobody meets a kid until it is done.
  • What happens to a kid between the last bell and dinner? For a lot of them, nothing, and nothing compounds. Our afternoon advocate blocks are purchasable in single-cycle units.
  • Do you have one hour on a Saturday you are not using well? A lot of our mentors did too. Child Champions Hub will match that hour with a kid who needs it. Free signup.
  • Reading a case file takes an advocate about four hours before they ever meet the child. Nobody volunteers for the four hours. They volunteer for what comes after, and we cover the training that makes those four hours useful.
  • Rain on a Saturday, and a mentor still drives out to a soccer field to watch a kid who might not play. Showing up like that is a habit we build with training and support calls. Your money pays for the habit, not the moment.
  • When a match falls apart, we pay for the repair and not just the start. That means a staff person sitting with a kid who has been let go of again, and a new search that takes months. Funding the repair work is the least popular ask we make and the most necessary.
  • Sponsorships here start small and stay useful. Any amount goes to the same three places: recruiting adults, training adults, keeping adults in the relationship. Tell us what you can commit to and we will match it to a real cost.
  • Court dates get moved and our advocates move with them. Rescheduling is not free, since somebody has to hold the calendar and drive the miles. Underwriting that flexibility is unglamorous and it is exactly where we need money.
  • No discount exists on training an adult to be trustworthy around a hurt kid. It takes weeks, and it costs what it costs. If your company wants to cover one volunteer's path from application to first meeting, we will show you the receipts.
  • I sat in on a match meeting in the spring and said almost nothing for an hour. Two adults spent that hour figuring out how a twelve year old could get to practice on Thursdays. Sponsoring one match pays for meetings like that, dull and necessary.
