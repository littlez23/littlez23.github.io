More ways real people are reaching further

  • How many of your better decisions trace back to an adult who told you the truth when it was inconvenient? Ours mostly do. That is the thing we are asking you to buy a year of.
  • What would thirteen-year-old you have needed most? Probably not advice. Probably somebody who kept turning up on a Thursday, and you can fund one of those Thursdays right now.
  • What is the last thing you gave to and never checked on again? No judgment, that is most giving. We would rather you check on us, and we will make it easy to.
  • What would you have done differently at fifteen with one honest adult in the room? We cannot answer that for you. We can put that adult in the room for someone else, if the seat is paid for.
  • Child Champions Hub has been operating in this county since before most people knew we existed. We are not new and we are not large. We are asking local businesses to help us keep the volunteer pipeline funded through next year.
  • Would twenty-five dollars a month be easier on you than a single larger gift? Some businesses prefer the small steady version because it does not need a decision every year. Either works on our end.
  • Plenty of our donors give a modest amount and read every update we send. We would take fifty of those over one anonymous windfall. Start wherever you like and tell us how much detail you want back.
  • Yesterday a mentor texted to ask whether we could cover a bus pass for her kid. We could, because a restaurant owner had given us a small unrestricted gift in March. Unrestricted money answers questions like that in an hour instead of a month.
  • Someone asks us monthly whether we take in-kind instead of cash, and often the answer is yes. Printing, meeting space, a fleet oil change. But cash is what pays the staff who hold the matches together, so we lead with that ask.
  • Small businesses tend to give better than large ones, in our experience, because the decision takes one conversation. We are having that conversation with about a dozen owners this month. If you want the numbers for a single sponsored match, say the word.
  • A girl in our program refuses to use her mentor's last name, only the first, because that is what friends do. Their pairing was funded by a local insurance office that asked for nothing back. We have room for one more arrangement like that.
  • Some of our strongest volunteers came from companies that wrote us a check first and got curious second. The money opened the door and then a person walked through it. Either order works for us, though the check is what keeps the lights on.
